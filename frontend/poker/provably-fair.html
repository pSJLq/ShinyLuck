Trust model

Provably fair · zkShuffle v2

How ShinyPoker makes the deal impossible to rig · and independently verifiable by anyone. Written straight, including what we don’t yet do.
The short version. Every cash table and every tournament runs full mental poker. The deck is ElGamal-encrypted under the table’s aggregate key and shuffled by the players’ own browsers; each shuffle carries a Wikström zero-knowledge proof that every other client verifies before it will release a decryption share; each card the room reads is decrypted from proven Chaum–Pedersen shares and checked by the contract to be exactly the card claimed. Your hole cards are decrypted on your machine only · no dealer service ever holds one. Watch the protocol run in real time in the zk Lab.
Archived: the v1 commit-reveal scheme (§2–§4). Before zkShuffle, the deck came from a sealed seed committed on-chain and bound to a future block hash, and an off-chain dealer derived it to serve hole cards. That version is retired · its contracts remain on-chain for withdrawals only. The sections below are kept as a record of what it did and where its trust boundary was, so the change is auditable rather than quietly rewritten.

1. What "maximally on-chain" means here

On ShinyPoker, the things that decide who wins money live on-chain (Somnia testnet today): your escrow balance, buy-ins, blinds and antes, every betting action (fold / check / call / bet / raise / all-in), the main pot and side-pots, rake, payouts, tournament registration and prize pools. Somnia's high throughput and sub-second finality let all of this happen without the table feeling slow. 1½. How the deal works today · zkShuffle v2

Nobody derives the deck, because nobody can. Each seated client generates a fresh per-hand keypair (the secret never leaves the tab) and proves knowledge of it with a Schnorr proof the contract verifies; the table key is the aggregate of those pubkeys, so a rogue key cannot join. The 52-card deck is then passed player to player: each one permutes and re-encrypts it and publishes a Wikström zero-knowledge argument that the output really is a permutation plus re-encryption of the input. Every other client fetches the full transcript, verifies all of it and pins its own stage inside it before it hands over a single decryption share. The deal commits the keccak of that whole transcript on-chain, so it stays re-verifiable forever.

From there, a card only becomes visible when the shares exist for it. Hole cards: everyone gives shares for other players' cards, so each player can finish their own decryption locally and nobody else can · the coordinator is one share short, by construction. Board cards: all shares are collected as each street closes, and revealBoardCard makes the contract check every Chaum–Pedersen proof and that the shares decrypt to exactly the claimed card. A forged share or a lied-about card reverts. A per-deal bitmask also rejects the same card value showing up twice.

If a tab dies mid-hand, the missing share becomes an on-chain accusation naming the exact card. The accused client watches the chain, not the bot, and can answer within a rescue window · which also delivers the withheld share, so stalling just walks a griefer through handing over every share one at a time. Only an accusation nobody answered lets the hand finalize with that player's committed chips forfeited, distributed pro-rata to the other players in the hand and never to the operator. Closing your tab in a lost pot costs you what folding would have.

What is still not proven inside a contract: the shuffle argument itself. It is verified by the coordinator and independently by every client, and the chain holds its commitment · not its verification. There is no third-party audit of the implementation yet. Both of those are stated here rather than in a footnote on purpose.

2. Archived · how the v1 deck was sealed (commit-reveal + block-hash entropy)

The deck for each hand is derived from two independent inputs, controlled by different parties, so neither the dealer nor anyone else can grind it into a favourable order:

  • A server seed, committed first. Before the hand, the hash keccak256(seed) is published on-chain. The hash reveals nothing about the seed, but locks it in · the seed can never be swapped later without the hash failing to match.
  • A future block hash, added after. Right after the hand starts, the deck is bound to the hash of an upcoming block · produced by Somnia's validators, unknown to the dealer when it committed the seed.

The 52-card deck is then a deterministic Fisher-Yates shuffle of seed + block-hash + hand id + table id. It's a pure function · the contract exposes recomputeDeck(...) so anyone can reproduce it bit-for-bit. Because no single party controls both inputs, no one can predict or steer the deck.

3. Archived · v1 reveal and verify

  • Commit (pre-deal). The seed hash is on-chain before any card exists. It can't change mid-hand.
  • Play. The board is posted street by street. Each posted board card is checked against the derived deck at reveal · a mismatch reverts the transaction.
  • Reveal (post-hand). The seed is revealed. The contract checks it hashes to the commitment, then re-derives the whole deck on-chain and ranks the hands from it. A dishonest board or hole card is impossible: the reveal transaction simply fails.
Every deal ID, seed commitment, revealed seed and block-hash entropy is on-chain via the dealer contract's dealInfo. Anyone can call recomputeDeck and confirm the cards they were shown match the committed shuffle · no trust required, and no permission needed.

3½. Verify a v1 hand yourself · right now, in this tab

This tool runs in your browser: it reads the deal from the chain, re-derives the whole deck locally from the revealed seed, cross-checks it against the contract's own recomputeDeck(), and diffs the board that was posted street by street. 4. Archived · the trust boundary v1 had, and v2 closed

In v1 an off-chain dealer service derived the deck (once the seed and block hash were known) to deliver each player their two hole cards. It could not cheat the result · cards, board and outcome were bound by the on-chain commitment · but because it derived the deck to serve it, it technically held every hand’s cards while the hand was live. That was a real trust point and we said so at the time.

That trust point is gone. On zkShuffle v2 there is no seed and no derivation: the coordinator relays ciphertexts and proofs it cannot decrypt, and it is always exactly one decryption share short of any player’s hole cards. It is not that we ask you to trust the dealer less · it holds nothing to be trusted with. See §1½, or watch it in the zk Lab.

5. Session keys (convenience without custody)

Signing a wallet transaction for every fold would be unusable. Instead, when you sit down you authorize a session key once, scoped to acting on your behalf at the table. Your actions are then signed locally and settle on-chain in the background · no popup per hand. You can revoke the session key at any time from Settings.

6. Live state

Table updates, timers and pot changes are served from a fast cache the dealer builds from on-chain state and delivers to your client, so hundreds of people can watch a table without each one hammering the chain. Everything that decides money is still settled on-chain.

7. What we will never do

  • Expose opponents' hidden cards to other players, or show fake "win % vs unknown hands".
  • Hold your funds in a custodial wallet · your balance is in escrow only you can withdraw. The contract has no path for the house to move a player's balance.
  • Hide rake or fees. Cash rake (with cap) and tournament fee splits are always shown up front.
  • Claim cryptography we haven't shipped. zkShuffle v2 is live · and until it was, this page said so, which is why §2 to §4 are still here instead of deleted.
