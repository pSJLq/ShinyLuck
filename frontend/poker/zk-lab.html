This page runs the v2 protocol core client-side on BN254 (the curve the EVM has native precompiles for), and every Schnorr key proof and Chaum–Pedersen decryption-share proof below is verified by a deployed contract (ZkDealerV2 on Somnia testnet) via a zero-gas eth_call · you're watching the actual chain accept or reject each proof. This same protocol deals every live table (the room's card layer is ZkTableDealer, wired to the on-chain verifier) · what you test here is what shuffles your hand. Not externally audited yet.

How it works, in one hand

Three players (simulated below, each with its own secret key your browser never shares between them): they aggregate a table key, each one shuffles and re-encrypts the deck themselves, then each hole card is decrypted only by its owner using the other players' decryption shares · and each of those shares is proven correct and checked by the contract on-chain. After the hand, everyone opens their shuffle · any tampering is caught, attributed, and provable on-chain (ZkDealerV2.checkStage). Watch the log: no party other than the owner ever holds a readable card.

What this stage does and doesn't prove

  • Does, on-chain: the aggregate table key (each player's Schnorr proof is verified by the contract), and every decryption share (Chaum–Pedersen, verified by the contract · a forged share is rejected by the chain). In-hand secrecy: your cards are ElGamal-encrypted under everyone's keys, so only you can complete the decryption. Cheat detection is deterministic and attributable, and the shuffle audit is re-derivable on-chain (checkStage).
  • Doesn't yet: zero-knowledge shuffle arguments (verifying each shuffle DURING the hand with nothing opened after) · stage R2 of the spec, replacing the optimistic audit; escrow slashing wired to the room; and an external audit. Deck storage on-chain is also currently heavy (~0.26 STT/hand) and will move to a hash-commit + calldata-verify model.
